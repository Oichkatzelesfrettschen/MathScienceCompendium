\chapter*{How to choose a route}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to choose a route}
The precalculus book supplies functions, algebra, trigonometry, vectors, and
elementary limits. These bridge chapters develop selected undergraduate
operations through definitions, examples, and solved exercises. The advanced
review then asks what mathematical, computational, and empirical evidence
establish. Its negative results teach why an appealing connection fails and
what evidence a stronger claim would require.

\section*{An entry check}
Before beginning, solve $x^2-5x+6=0$, compute $\sin(\pi/6)$, and multiply
$\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}3\\4\end{pmatrix}$.
The answers are $x=2,3$, $1/2$, and $(11,4)^T$. Explain each operation,
including why the quadratic factorization gives both roots. If an answer
requires a guess, use the precalculus sections on polynomials, the unit
circle, or matrices before following the corresponding branch.

\section*{How history and evidence appear}
Historical cards identify documented contexts and distinguish sources from
modern teaching interpretations. Modern notation and constructed exercises
belong to the exposition. The audit record identifies inaccessible sources;
a citation alone establishes neither access nor factual verification.
