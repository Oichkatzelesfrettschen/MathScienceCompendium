ABSTRACT/SUMMARY:
================================================================================

This lecture covers thermal conductivity in solids from the perspective of
phonon transport. It begins with a review of phonons as particles, comparing
classical molecular gases to phonon gases. The lecture develops the theory of
thermal conductivity coefficient and its dependence on heat capacity,
particle velocity, and mean free path.

Key concept: Thermal conductivity K = (1/3) C v l, where C is heat capacity
per unit volume, v is average particle velocity, and l is mean free path.

================================================================================
KEY MATHEMATICAL CLAIMS:
================================================================================

1. THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY DEFINITION:
   j_U = -K (dT/dx)
   where:
   - j_U = energy transmitted across unit area per unit time
   - K = thermal conductivity coefficient
   - dT/dx = temperature gradient
   - Negative sign: heat flows from hot to cold

2. THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY COEFFICIENT:
   K = (1/3) C v l
   where:
   - C = heat capacity (constant volume, per unit volume)
   - v = average particle velocity
   - l = mean free path between collisions

3. PHONON OCCUPATION NUMBER (BOSE-EINSTEIN):
   <n> = 1 / (exp(h-bar*omega/(k_B*T)) - 1)
   Limits:
   - Small T: <n> ~ exp(-h-bar*omega/(k_B*T)) (exponential suppression)
   - Large T: <n> ~ k_B*T/(h-bar*omega) (classical limit)

4. PHONON DISPERSION (ACOUSTIC BRANCH NEAR k=0):
   omega ~ v_s * k
   where v_s = speed of sound, k = crystal momentum

5. INTERNAL ENERGY:
   Classical gas (N particles): U = 3 N k_B T
   Phonon gas: U = sum over (K,p) of <n_(K,p)> * h-bar*omega_(K,p)
            = sum over (K,p) of (h-bar*omega_(K,p))/(exp(h-bar*omega/(k_B*T))-1)

6. ENERGY TRANSPORT PER PARTICLE:
   Delta_U = C * Delta_T
   where C is heat capacity, Delta_T is temperature difference

================================================================================
KEY PHYSICAL CLAIMS:
================================================================================

1. PHONONS AS PARTICLE GAS:
   - Can treat quantized lattice vibrations as particle gas
   - Analogous to classical molecular gas but with key differences

2. PHONON GAS VS CLASSICAL GAS DIFFERENCES:
   - Number: Classical gas fixed, phonon population temperature-dependent
   - Energy-momentum: Classical KE=p^2/(2m), phonons omega~v_s*k
   - Collisions: Both conserve energy; phonon momentum conserved modulo
     reciprocal lattice vector G
   - Particle number: Conserved in classical gas, NOT conserved for phonons

3. HEAT FLOW IS INCOHERENT:
   - Temperature changes associated with entropy changes
   - Not ballistic process, involves many collisions
   - Diffusive transport mechanism

4. TEMPERATURE DETERMINES PHONON POPULATION:
   - Higher temperature -> higher occupation of phonon modes
   - Modes with energy < or comparable to temperature preferentially occupied

5. REAL-WORLD THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY:
   - Large pan on small burner: slow edge heating (finite thermal conductivity)
   - Different materials at same temperature feel different (marble cooler
     than wood due to higher thermal conductivity)
   - Electronics cooling: high thermal conductivity materials draw heat from
     processor

6. PHONON SCATTERING MECHANISMS:
   - Phonon-phonon collisions
   - Surface scattering at crystal boundaries
   - Impurity scattering

RESULTS/KEY INSIGHTS:
================================================================================

1. THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY FORMULA:
   K = (1/3) C v l provides simple physical picture

2. THREE FACTORS CONTROL K:
   - Heat capacity C (energy storage per temperature change)
   - Velocity v (transport speed)
   - Mean free path l (distance between scattering events)

3. PHONON POPULATION LIMITS:
   - Low T: exponentially suppressed occupation
   - High T: classical behavior (equipartition)

4. MOMENTUM CONSERVATION SUBTLETY:
   - Phonon momentum conserved modulo reciprocal lattice vector
   - Allows Umklapp processes (momentum non-conserving in real space)

5. PARTICLE NUMBER NON-CONSERVATION:
   - Phonons can be created/destroyed
   - Distinguishes phonon gas from classical molecular gas
